# War of Attrition — Private Narrative Canon & Disclosure Plan

Status: **Authoritative creative specification for writers and developers.**

This document contains the objective history of the setting, including facts the player should not receive directly. It is the source of truth for narrative implementation. [`north-star.md`](./north-star.md) remains authoritative for tone and visual identity; [`opponent-commanders.md`](./opponent-commanders.md) remains authoritative for the implemented fair-play AI strategies; and [`alternate-rules-campaigns.md`](./alternate-rules-campaigns.md) remains authoritative for campaign mechanics. The implementation-ready reveal routing and authored encounter order live in [`narrative-disclosure-matrix.md`](./narrative-disclosure-matrix.md); final voice and curated copy live in [`commander-voice-bible.md`](./commander-voice-bible.md).

> [!CAUTION]
> The mouse, disturbed hay, and natural origin of the Witness Wheel's eyes are **private canon**. Do not state this answer in ordinary player-facing copy, especially in Chapters I or II. The story is designed to be reconstructed from sincere testimony, contradictions, records, prophecy, and optional real-world context.

## 1. Narrative Method

War of Attrition does not need a conventional dialogue-heavy story mode. Its story should emerge across repeated play through commander identities and biographies, campaign framing, contextual table reactions, mode descriptions, Field Manual material, Chronicle fragments, achievements, cosmetics, and other existing or low-cost surfaces.

The complete story is not narrated up front:

- Characters provide sincere but incomplete and sometimes self-serving versions of history.
- Later fragments change the meaning of earlier lines.
- Campaign mode can change what a commander is willing or able to say.
- Optional links may lead into real historical, culinary, scientific, legal, or linguistic context.
- The interface remains dignified and straight-faced; the world supplies the absurdity.
- Story content must not obscure cards, delay rapid play, or turn the game into a visual novel.

The development team must distinguish three things in every piece of narrative data:

1. **Objective canon** — what actually happened.
2. **Character knowledge** — what a character directly knows or experienced.
3. **Character belief or presentation** — what the character concludes, claims, rationalizes, or omits.

Preserve the two creative maxims:

> **The cards do not merely represent the battle. They summon the battle.**

> **Provide context, not punchline annotations.**

## 2. Historical and Scientific Inspiration

The Mont-Rouge Affair is fictional. It is not a claim about a documented modern Gruyère incident, and the setting is not a literal retelling of current regulation.

The following real-world echoes are approved foundations:

- Cheese production in the Swiss Gruyère region has documentary roots in the medieval period. The Interprofession du Gruyère dates the tradition to 1115 and describes cheese from the region being traded into France and Italy.
- Cheesemaking knowledge and production crossed into neighboring France. French and Swiss producer histories both describe a connected, cross-border tradition, while differing in emphasis.
- French and Swiss interests have genuinely negotiated and disputed use and protection of the Gruyère name. French and Swiss producer bodies made a joint declaration of intent in 2004 to protect Gruyère before the present protection systems diverged.
- French Gruyère IGP requires characteristic openings ranging roughly from pea to cherry size.
- Swiss Le Gruyère AOP belongs to a much more closed or "blind" visual tradition. Its official characteristics allow a few small openings but do not require them. Do not simplify this into the false claim that every authentic Swiss wheel must contain literally no opening.
- Research on Swiss-type cheeses supports the scientific inspiration for the fictional accident: eye formation can begin at microscopic structural nuclei containing trapped air, and work by Agroscope discusses hay particles with capillary structures as such nuclei. This does **not** establish that the fictional mouse/hay sequence happened to any historical Gruyère wheel.

## 3. Objective Private Chronology: The Mont-Rouge Affair

This chronology is immutable unless this document is deliberately revised.

1. For generations, French and Swiss Gruyère guilds feud over provenance, naming rights, production tradition, and the visible difference between open/eyed and blind/closed cheese.
2. **Marcel de Brie**, representing French interests, and **Matthias von Greyerz**, representing Swiss interests, become respected professional rivals. They find one another infuriating but fundamentally honorable. Their prior trust is essential.
3. Marcel and Matthias negotiate the **Mont-Rouge Accord**. It recognizes both traditions' ancient relationship to the protected name while permitting distinct standards.
4. Ceremonial Witness Wheels are produced for ratification. The French wheel is characteristically open. The Swiss wheel should be essentially blind.
5. Months before ratification, an insignificant field mouse enters the loft or storage space associated with the Swiss dairy and disturbs dry hay or similar plant material. Tiny structural material enters the cheesemaking environment unnoticed.
6. The Swiss Witness Wheel ages. At Mont-Rouge it is opened before the assembled delegations and contains unexpected eyes.
7. There was no sabotage, switched wheel, bribed monk, deliberate contamination, or betrayal. **No betrayal occurred.**
8. Humiliated and unable to explain the wheel, Matthias asks to suspend ratification pending investigation.
9. Marcel experiences the suspension as betrayal. He had staked his reputation on Matthias's good faith and believes Matthias has withdrawn when called upon to stand beside him.
10. Marcel's English confidant, **Sir Edmund Gloucester**, argues that Swiss hesitation must not destroy years of negotiation. Edmund deliberately goads Marcel to call what he considers a bluff.
11. Marcel proceeds unilaterally with French recognition or ratification under the negotiated framework. He knows this is a choice and understands that Switzerland may see it as a hostile fait accompli.
12. Matthias experiences Marcel's unilateral action as betrayal: Marcel appears to exploit an inexplicable ceremonial failure the instant Matthias requests time to investigate it.
13. Matthias's Italian confidant, **Lorenzo di Taleggio**, treats Marcel's action as confirmation of expansionist intent and gives worst-case possibilities the emotional force of probabilities.
14. Swiss authorities close or restrict an Alpine pass and impound disputed shipments.
15. France sends escorts or otherwise acts to reopen or secure the route.
16. Opposing forces meet. A confrontation produces the first genuine bloodshed.
17. Every retaliation becomes retroactive evidence that the preceding side had been treacherous all along.
18. The War of Attrition grows from a betrayal that never happened.

## 4. Commander Identities and Dossiers

These five fictional identities map onto the five permanent strategy IDs. Names may receive minor editorial refinement only if an actual repository constraint requires it. Do not add a strategy or a sixth Tyromancer commander for narrative convenience.

| Character | Strategy ID | Faction | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marcel de Brie | `quartermaster` | French | Master affineur and principal French negotiator of the Accord |
| Sir Edmund Gloucester | `gambler` | English | Eccentric artisan-adventurer and Marcel's confidant |
| Matthias von Greyerz | `analyst` | Swiss | Master cheesemaker and principal Swiss negotiator of the Accord |
| Bastien de Herve | `attritionist` | Belgian | Apolitical tyromancer, itinerant rind-seer, and apparently mad prophet |
| Lorenzo di Taleggio | `cornered-general` | Italian | Alpine merchant-prince, cheesemaker, and Matthias's political confidant |

The IDs and their parameterized fair-play behavior remain authoritative in code. Fiction changes their names, biographies, and voices; it does not change their access to information, cards, or rules.

### Marcel de Brie — `quartermaster`

**Knows:** He negotiated sincerely; the Swiss Witness Wheel unexpectedly contained eyes; no evidence connected his delegation to tampering; Matthias suspended ratification; Marcel then chose to proceed without him.

**Believes:** Matthias sacrificed the agreement rather than endure humiliation before the Swiss guilds. Later Swiss actions become confirmation that the withdrawal was intentional.

**Withholds or distorts:** Marcel claims he had no choice but to proceed. He did. Fear that the Accord would collapse, wounded pride, and anger led him to force the issue despite understanding how the Swiss would interpret it.

**Relationships:** Matthias is a respected rival and perceived betrayer. Edmund is a trusted ally and dangerously effective encourager. Lorenzo is an opportunistic whisperer in Matthias's ear. Bastien is irritating because nonsense occasionally touches uncomfortable truths.

### Matthias von Greyerz — `analyst`

**Knows:** The Witness Wheel was prepared under Swiss control; its openings were genuinely unexpected; he had no evidence identifying a saboteur; he suspended ratification; Marcel proceeded without him.

**Believes:** Marcel knew more than he admitted or seized an astonishingly convenient accident for political gain. The unilateral ratification hardens suspicion into certainty.

**Withholds or distorts:** Matthias presents his response as purely rational procedure. Humiliation and immediate suspicion of Marcel influenced calculations he later calls objective.

**Relationships:** Marcel is the rival whose opinion matters more than Matthias admits. Lorenzo articulates every danger Matthias can calculate. Edmund is a reckless provocateur. Bastien offends Matthias's worldview because impossible predictions keep becoming correct.

### Sir Edmund Gloucester — `gambler`

**Knows:** No evidence showed Matthias sabotaged the wheel; Marcel genuinely trusted Matthias; the suspension deeply wounded Marcel.

**Believes:** Hesitation is itself a decision. Diplomacy, cards, and war favor the person willing to act while others continue calculating.

**Withholds or distorts:** Edmund later describes himself as merely supporting Marcel. He deliberately goaded Marcel into calling what he considered a Swiss bluff. He wanted decisive action; he did not want or expect war. That distinction becomes increasingly important to him.

**Relationships:** He is loyal to Marcel, finds Matthias maddeningly cautious, sees Lorenzo as another player at the table, and initially treats Bastien's prophecies as marvelous entertainment.

### Lorenzo di Taleggio — `cornered-general`

**Knows:** There was no direct proof against Marcel; a unilateral French move could genuinely threaten Swiss bargaining power and Alpine trade.

**Believes:** Security disappears one concession at a time. Waiting until hostile intent is undeniable means waiting until one is already cornered.

**Withholds or distorts:** Lorenzo says he only explained possible risks. He repeatedly presented worst cases with the emotional force of imminent probabilities because he wanted Matthias to act before becoming cornered. He is an accelerant, not a mastermind.

**Relationships:** He is loyal to Matthias, distrusts Marcel's reach, views Edmund as irresponsible while performing a mirror-image role, and dismisses Bastien until doing so becomes difficult.

### Bastien de Herve — `attritionist`

Bastien is both the Belgian commander and the Tyromancer.

**Knows through prophecy:** There was no traitor; something tiny brought "the field" into the blind wheel; Marcel and Matthias will mistake one another's defensible acts for betrayal; Edmund and Lorenzo will worsen matters while believing themselves loyal; the dispute will become war. He did not secretly investigate the dairy. The cheese told him.

**Believes:** Prophecy may warn people without giving them the wisdom to understand the warning.

**Withholds or distorts:** Bastien does **not** lie about Mont-Rouge. He is the only major character who consistently tells the truth. His apparent unreliability comes from absurd tyromantic riddles and from unrelated predictions that need not all be useful.

**Voice:** Prophetic motifs may include the blind opening its eyes, a field growing where no grass grows, two honest men calling one another liar, the smallest guest commanding the greatest army, a betrayal committed by nobody, or a war whose first casualty was certainty. Treat these as a motif palette, not a stock list to repeat verbatim. His long-horizon Attritionist behavior suits someone who acts as though he has already seen the campaign's end.

**Relationships:** He is politically aligned with neither side. Marcel and Matthias find him differently intolerable; Edmund begins amused; Lorenzo begins dismissive. All four gradually discover that Bastien has been maddeningly consistent.

## 6. Four-Chapter Disclosure Structure

The canonical order is:

> **Standard → Limited Reserves → Fog of War → Total War**

Each chapter is one completed Three-War Campaign in its corresponding mode. Completion—not victory—unlocks the next chapter. Narrative access must never depend on skill, tokens, purchases, achievements, advertising, or monetization. Once unlocked, a mode remains available for replay.

For the canonical first-play progression, opponent identity is authored at the **War** level:

| Chapter | War I | War II | War III |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — The Accord | Marcel de Brie | Matthias von Greyerz | Bastien de Herve |
| II — The Closing Passes | Sir Edmund Gloucester | Lorenzo di Taleggio | Marcel de Brie |
| III — The Blind Wheel | Matthias von Greyerz | Marcel de Brie | Bastien de Herve |
| IV — The War of Attrition | Sir Edmund Gloucester | Lorenzo di Taleggio | Matthias von Greyerz |

Do not preserve the current one-commander-per-Campaign implementation as a narrative constraint. Initial replays may reuse this schedule. Randomized replay scheduling is desirable only as a deferred pre-production polish pass after the canonical four-chapter progression has been completed at least once; first-play comprehension must not depend on random order.

### Chapter I — Standard Campaign: “The Accord”

**Purpose:** Establish the surface story.

**May reveal:** The ancient French/Swiss rivalry; Marcel and Matthias; the Mont-Rouge Accord; the Witness Wheels; the inexplicable Swiss openings; failed negotiations; sincere accusations of betrayal; Bastien's truthful but incomprehensible warnings.

**Must not reveal:** The natural cause; the mouse or hay; the full sequence of escalation; that neither side had evidence.

**Intended player conclusion:** “Something happened at Mont-Rouge, and one of these men probably betrayed the other.”

### Chapter II — Limited Reserves: “The Closing Passes”

**Purpose:** Make scarcity and reserve anxiety resonate with the escalation.

**May reveal:** Restricted passes; impounded shipments; shortages; Marcel's unilateral move; Matthias's response; Edmund and Lorenzo as parallel hawkish confidants; reciprocal defenses becoming evidence of aggression.

**Must preserve:** Both principals become more sympathetic, not less. Edmund and Lorenzo must not become masterminds.

**Intended player conclusion:** “Both sides had reasons for what they did, but somebody still must have started this.”

### Chapter III — Fog of War: “The Blind Wheel”

**Purpose:** Make incomplete historical knowledge the subject of the chapter as well as its mechanic.

**May reveal:** Conflicting Mont-Rouge accounts; the absence of sabotage evidence; inconsistent testimony; mundane dairy or abbey records; references to vermin; disturbed or replaced straw/hay; scientifically grounded fragments about natural eye formation and structural nuclei; earlier prophecies gaining retrospective meaning.

**Must not do:** Introduce a detective who explains the answer. The fragments and optional sources should make the natural explanation inferable, not announce it.

**Intended player conclusion:** “Oh no. There might never have been a betrayal.”

### Chapter IV — Total War: “The War of Attrition”

**Purpose:** Deliver the emotional and thematic conclusion: every accumulated action matters.

**May reveal:** The human cost; public certainty covering private doubt; Edmund's admission that he pushed Marcel to act; Lorenzo's admission that he gave worst cases the force of imminent threats; both allies realizing loyalty worsened the disaster; Marcel and Matthias confronting the absence of proof; Bastien remaining consistent with everything he has said.

**Must preserve:** The conclusion need not restore friendship or produce a tidy peace. Humor may remain, but the tone can become more restrained. No principal is revealed as the original saboteur because there was no sabotage.

**Intended player conclusion:** “They destroyed each other over a betrayal committed by nobody.”

## 7. Unlock and Migration Contract for Sprint 1

The current production schema does **not** implement chapter locks. It exposes all four mode options at the start of every fresh Campaign. Sprint 1 must add progression without invalidating existing campaigns or revoking access players already had.

Required behavior:

1. New profiles created under the narrative schema begin with Standard unlocked.
2. Completing all three Wars of an unlocked chapter records completion and unlocks the next chapter regardless of wins, losses, ties, or Campaign outcome.
3. Unlocked modes remain replayable. Narrative progression chooses availability, not a forced one-time mode sequence.
4. An in-progress legacy Campaign keeps its existing `mode`, recorded Wars, reserves, selected-orders state, and current opponent identity through migration. Migration must not reroll the active opponent; new unplayed Wars can join the authored schedule only where doing so preserves continuity.
5. Migration should infer the highest reached chapter from the active Campaign and `recentCampaigns`, then unlock that chapter and all prerequisites.
6. Because the old schema offered every mode to every profile and has no reliable “previously viewed but never played” record, profiles that predate the narrative schema should be sensibly grandfathered rather than stripped of choices. The safest default is full four-mode access for demonstrably legacy profiles unless a reliable access signal is introduced before release.
7. Do not infer narrative access from tokens, cosmetics, achievements, career win rate, or telemetry.
8. Persist only the minimal chapter completion/unlock state and any genuinely necessary major narrative flags. Bump and normalize the progression schema; do not overload `ordersSelected` to mean chapter access.

The Campaign Orders UI should eventually distinguish locked, newly unlocked, completed, and replayable chapters. That is Sprint 1 implementation work, not part of this documentation pass.

## 8. Narrative Surfaces and Smallest Useful Architecture

Use existing surfaces before adding new presentation systems:

| Surface | Current implementation seam | Narrative use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commander identity | `OpponentCommander` registry, Campaign Orders, table seat, profile/telemetry | Name, title, faction, chapter-aware characterization, and the actual opponent for the current War. The identity block should deep-link to the progressive dossier. |
| Contextual reactions | `OpponentCommanderDialogue` + `TableReactionService` | Brief event- and mode-aware lines; silence remains the default. |
| Campaign Orders | Pre-War-1 modal with all four modes | Chapter title, compact framing, lock/completion status, mode resonance, and the three authored opponents in War order. |
| Chronicle | `StoryBookService` in-memory tactical event feed | Short contextual fragments attached to meaningful events; do not obscure truthful combat records. |
| Field Manual | `StoryBookDrawerComponent` tab architecture | Progressive commander dossiers, durable lore/archival fragments, and optional source links. Dossiers improve historiography as chapters advance; they are not omniscient character sheets. |
| Hall of Valor | Profile-persistent card service records in the Field Manual | Sparse flavor where a real card's history can echo campaign lore; never rewrite its statistics. |
| War/Campaign resolution | Existing game-over and campaign progression events | One short reaction or dispatch; do not force long interstitials on replay. |
| Achievements/cosmetics | Existing persistent descriptions and card backs | Optional historical fragments whose meaning changes after later chapters. |

The present dialogue contract is a fixed set of event pools and is not mode-aware. Sprint 1 may extend it with a small, data-driven condition shape supporting at least:

- commander ID;
- campaign mode/chapter;
- War index in the authored encounter schedule;
- broad chapter progression;
- meaningful gameplay event;
- optionally, prior chapter completion or a very small set of major narrative flags.

Prefer keyed pools or filtered line records over a general-purpose branching dialogue engine. Narrative data must not gain access to hidden card information. Avoid persistent transcript logs, quest graphs, dialogue trees, affinity scores, or RPG statistics. Progressive dossier entries may add, supplement, or annotate a small historical record as evidence becomes available; their authored plan is in [`narrative-disclosure-matrix.md`](./narrative-disclosure-matrix.md).

## 9. Rabbit-Hole Plan

Candidate subjects include Gruyère history; Franco-Swiss naming and protection disputes; AOP/IGP/appellation systems; French openings; cheese eye formation; affineurs; Alpine transhumance; tyromancy; Herve cheese; cheese as storage and trade of seasonal milk; cheese riots and trade disputes; and Cooper's Hill cheese rolling where it enriches Edmund.

A linked fact should ideally do five jobs:

1. enrich a line or description;
2. deepen a character;
3. support the fictional history;
4. reward knowledgeable players;
5. let curious players discover a real analogue.

| Research subject | First source target | Likely narrative job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gruyère history and cross-border protection | Swiss/French producer bodies, INAO, Swiss intellectual-property records, and EU GI records | Foundation for Marcel, Matthias, the Accord, and rival standards |
| French openings and Swiss closed texture | Product specifications and regulator/producer characteristics | The visible contradiction at Mont-Rouge |
| Eye formation and structural nuclei | Agroscope and peer-reviewed dairy science | Chapter III fragments that make the natural cause inferable |
| Affineurs and maturation | Producer organizations, dairy museums, and culinary institutions | Marcel's craft vocabulary and reserve metaphors |
| Alpine transhumance and seasonal milk storage/trade | UNESCO, Alpine museums, universities, and regional historical societies | Passes, pasture rights, logistics, and why cheese bears political weight |
| AOP, IGP, PDO, and appellation law | INAO, EU GI registers, and Swiss intellectual-property institutions | The Accord's legal and guild vocabulary |
| Tyromancy | Historical dictionaries, digitized primary texts, museum/university folklore collections | Bastien's sincere prophetic practice without validating gameplay magic |
| Herve cheese | Official PDO/GI records and Belgian producer or regional institutions | Bastien's Belgian identity and rind vocabulary |
| Cheese riots and trade disputes | Municipal museums, archives, and historical societies | Evidence that absurd food disputes can have material stakes |
| Cooper's Hill cheese rolling | Gloucestershire local institutions and well-sourced historical overviews | Edmund's appetite for decisive, hazardous action |

Links belong in biographies, Field Manual lore, optional Chronicle detail, campaign dispatches, and item descriptions—not in mandatory pop-ups or core card controls.

Source order for precision:

1. official producer or regulatory specification;
2. museum, university, historical society, or primary/institutional source;
3. reputable scholarly work;
4. Wikipedia or a similarly approachable overview for broad discovery.

Do not turn the interface into a citation manager and do not use a source link to explain why a joke is funny.

## 10. Narrative Guardrails

- Do not change core card rules, commander strategy IDs, fair-play AI, telemetry/privacy boundaries, physical-deck fidelity, accessibility, or mobile readability.
- Do not give commanders magical gameplay abilities or hidden-information access.
- Do not create collectible stat cards, combat stats, or a general RPG dialogue system.
- Do not add a sixth commander or separate Bastien from the Tyromancer role.
- Do not explicitly reveal the mouse/hay answer in early player-facing copy.
- Do not make Edmund or Lorenzo masterminds, Marcel or Matthias secretly evil, or either principal the original saboteur.
- Do not make Bastien a conventional omniscient narrator or align him politically with either side.
- Do not explain every joke.
- Do not let narrative fragments delay repeated play or compromise card readability.
